Research topic 02

Simulation of Sodium-Ion Battery Interface Kinetics

We use reactive molecular dynamics and rare-event sampling to follow the chemistry at the interface between electrode and electrolyte in sodium-ion batteries: how sodium enters the electrode, and how the protective interphase layer forms.

Background

Sodium-ion batteries are a promising complement to lithium-ion batteries. Sodium is abundant and inexpensive, which lowers cost and supply risks, but sodium-ion batteries still lag behind in energy density and cycling stability.

Black phosphorus is a promising anode material: it can store a large amount of sodium and conducts well, but it expands by up to 300% during sodiation, and how it stores sodium is not yet fully understood. Just as important is the solid electrolyte interphase (SEI), a thin layer of electrolyte decomposition products that forms on the anode during the first cycles. It lets ions through while protecting the electrolyte from further breakdown, but in sodium-ion batteries it is more complex and less stable than in lithium-ion batteries. Both processes happen at the molecular scale and on timescales that experiments can hardly resolve.

Our approach

We simulate these processes with reactive molecular dynamics, using the ReaxFF and eReaxFF force fields. Unlike classical force fields, they allow chemical bonds to break and form, and eReaxFF also describes the transfer of electrons that drives the decomposition of the electrolyte. Many of the relevant events, such as a single decomposition reaction, are too rare to be observed in a plain simulation. We therefore combine the simulations with replica exchange transition interface sampling (RETIS), using the open-source software PyRETIS, to obtain reaction mechanisms and rates.

We are currently establishing and validating this workflow on lithium-based electrolytes, with the ring-opening of ethylene carbonate as a test case. Building on it, the work continues with:

  • Force field parametrization: robust ReaxFF and eReaxFF parameters for sodium-based systems with black phosphorus electrodes and organic solvent mixtures.
  • Sodium insertion: the mechanism by which sodium enters the phosphorene sheets of black phosphorus.
  • SEI formation: how the solid electrolyte interphase forms at the electrode–electrolyte interface.

Applications

Knowing the reaction pathways and rates at the interface helps explain how sodium-ion batteries age, and can guide the choice of electrolyte composition and electrode structure for longer-lasting cells. The rates obtained with RETIS can also serve as input for larger-scale models of SEI growth.
